**Incident Post‑Mortem – Checkout Platform**  
*Date of Incident: 2026‑09‑18 14:32 UTC – 2026‑09‑18 18:07 UTC*  

---

### 1. Summary  
A multi‑service degradation impacted the checkout experience. The incident began when Kubernetes nodes in the `checkout-prod` namespace entered **DiskPressure**. This triggered pod evictions and CrashLoopBackOff for eight critical checkout pods, cascading failures across Redis, Kafka, RabbitMQ, PostgreSQL, S3, and external payment services. The result was a 30 % increase in checkout failures, 503 errors from Stripe, and a 480,000‑message consumer lag in Kafka.

---

### 2. Root Cause  
**Primary:**  
- **Kubernetes DiskPressure** on two nodes caused eviction of essential checkout pods.  
- Evicted pods led to high Redis memory usage, session store latency, and OAuth token refresh failures.  
- Stalled pods slowed Kafka consumers and RabbitMQ processing, creating large consumer lags and unacknowledged message backlogs.

**Secondary (downstream):**  
- PostgreSQL replication lag (210 s) and S3 throttling (503 SlowDown) compounded the instability but were consequences of the initial node disk exhaustion.

*Root‑cause confidence: 0.93*

---

### 3. Recommended Fix  
1. **Persistent Storage** – Attach local SSD persistent volume claims to all checkout pods to isolate disk usage from node storage.  
2. **Node Auto‑Scaling** – Enable cluster autoscaler with disk‑usage thresholds (e.g., 80 % usage) to add nodes before DiskPressure occurs.  
3. **Eviction Policy** – Configure a disk‑pressure eviction policy that prioritizes non‑critical pods, preventing critical checkout pods from being evicted.  
4. **Monitoring & Alerts** – Add Prometheus alerts for node disk usage and pod eviction events; ensure Grafana dashboards reflect real‑time disk metrics.

*Fix confidence: 0.92*
